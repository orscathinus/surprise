HADEAN
01
4.54–4.0 billion years ago

Hadean

Earth begins as an impact-heated world. Metal sinks toward the core, silicate rock differentiates above it, and a giant collision early in the planet's history helps produce the Moon. As the surface cools, liquid water can persist.

  • Surface: magma, new crust, intense volcanism
  • Atmosphere: water vapor, CO₂, nitrogen; no breathable oxygen
  • Evidence: ancient zircon crystals preserve chemical clues from very early crust
02
4.0–2.5 billion years ago

Archean

Small continents and volcanic islands rise above dark oceans. Microbial ecosystems flourish. Photosynthetic microbes begin a planetary chemical revolution whose atmospheric consequences will take hundreds of millions of years to unfold.

  • Dominant life: microbes
  • Sun: substantially fainter than today
  • Signature: stromatolites record layered microbial communities
03
2.5 billion–538.8 million years ago

Proterozoic

Oxygen rises, complex cells diversify, and multicellular organisms appear. Severe Neoproterozoic glaciations leave evidence for ice at low latitudes. Near the end, soft-bodied Ediacaran organisms occupy seafloors before the Cambrian radiation.

  • Planetary shift: oxygenation
  • Climate extreme: Snowball Earth episodes
  • Biology: eukaryotes and multicellularity expand
04
538.8–251.9 million years ago

Paleozoic

Animal diversity expands in the seas. Plants and arthropods colonize land, forests alter soils and carbon cycling, fishes diversify, and tetrapods move onto land. The era ends with the most severe extinction of the Phanerozoic.

  • Icon: trilobites
  • Innovation: jaws, limbs, forests, amniotic eggs
  • Finale: end-Permian mass extinction
05
251.9–66 million years ago

Mesozoic

Dinosaurs dominate many terrestrial ecosystems while marine reptiles patrol the oceans and pterosaurs occupy the air. Birds arise within theropod dinosaurs, flowering plants diversify, and Pangaea breaks apart. An asteroid impact closes the era.

  • Continents: Pangaea fragments
  • Innovation: feathers, birds, flowering plants
  • Boundary: K–Pg impact and extinction
06
66 million years ago–now

Cenozoic

Mammals and birds rapidly diversify after the K–Pg extinction. Global climate cools over tens of millions of years, grasslands expand, and repeated ice ages reshape the recent world. One lineage of apes eventually reconstructs the entire story.

  • Climate arc: greenhouse warmth → long cooling trend
  • Familiar life: modern mammal and bird lineages diversify
  • Late arrival: Homo sapiens appears around 300,000 years ago

04 / HOW WE KNOW

The past left
receipts.

Deep time is reconstructed from physical traces. Open each evidence drawer to see what scientists measure, what it can tell us, and where uncertainty enters.

RADIOMETRIC DATING

Zircon: a microscopic clock

Zircon crystals incorporate uranium when they form but strongly reject lead. Over time, uranium isotopes decay to lead at known rates.

WHAT IT REVEALS

Ages of igneous rocks and tiny surviving crystals from Earth's earliest crust.

CAUTION

A date usually records a specific mineral-forming event, not automatically the age of every surrounding feature.

Hominin evolution, global migration, agriculture, cities, writing, industry, and spaceflight all fit into a geological sliver. The astonishing fact is not that humanity is unimportant—it is how much happened in so little time.
